# Brand Profile — Leadership MCP
<!-- version: 1.0 · last updated: 2026-10-09 · review: quarterly or on rebrand/pivot/new offer · type: b2b-saas -->

## Identity
- **What it is (one sentence):** Leadership MCP is an open-source MCP server that brings humanist leadership guidance into the moment someone needs to handle a difficult conversation.
- **Category:** Open-source AI/developer tool for leadership practice and continuous learning reinforcement.
- **Founded / location:** Created and maintained by Mario Campello; distributed globally with an initial PT-BR market focus.

## Positioning
- **What's different:** It does not dump leadership theory after the fact. The assistant detects a relational situation, asks whether the person wants help, and uses a living knowledge base to shape a concrete response before action is taken.
- **Why believe it:** MIT-licensed open-source project; installable locally through npm; compatible with MCP clients; knowledge base is inspectable markdown; deterministic/offline-first classifier; 40/40 evaluation cases passing in the repository smoke test.
- **Point of view:**
  - Leadership training evaporates when it is separated from the interaction where the behavior matters.
  - AI should offer a hypothesis and preserve human choice, not act as an authority over relationships.
  - Open-source tools should be useful, inspectable and respectful of sensitive context.
- **Won't say:** It is not therapy, a replacement for judgment, a guarantee of better relationships, or an official product of Anthropic, Barry-Wehmiller, Bob Chapman or Simon Sinek.

## Audience
### Segment 1 — Leadership facilitators and people consultants
- **Who:** Independent facilitators, executive coaches, leadership-training companies and people/culture consultants serving organizations.
- **Cares about:** Behavior transfer after workshops, measurable reinforcement, differentiation of their methodology and practical tools clients actually use.
- **Believes / objects:** They believe good training needs practice; they may object that AI flattens nuance, exposes sensitive data or weakens their methodology.
- **Where:** LinkedIn, leadership and HR communities, specialist newsletters, meetups and one-to-one professional networks.
- **Sophistication:** Expert in leadership practice; beginner to intermediate in MCP and AI infrastructure.
### Segment 2 — People-facing AI users and builders
- **Who:** Managers, founders, HRBPs, tech leads, PMs and AI/open-source practitioners who use Claude or compatible assistants.
- **Cares about:** Responding well under pressure, reducing escalation, practical AI utility, privacy and easy installation.
- **Believes / objects:** They know they should pause before reacting; they may object that the tool is abstract, preachy or difficult to install.
- **Where:** GitHub, MCP Brasil, TabNews, AI/automation communities, technical meetups and Facebook/LinkedIn groups.
- **Sophistication:** Mixed; content must explain the human use case first and the MCP mechanics second.
- **Buyer vs follower note:** Facilitators and consultancies are the potential B2B2C buyers/distributors; practitioners and builders are the early users, sharers and contributors.
- **Transformation:** Before: training or good intentions disappear under pressure. After: the person pauses, sees the relational dimension and chooses a concrete next step with more care.

- **We sound calm, never cold; direct, never aggressive; thoughtful, never preachy; human, never sentimental; technically credible, never jargon-heavy.**
- **Words we use:** momento da verdade, conversa difícil, hipótese de ação, você decide, liderança humanista, base viva, reforço contínuo, comportamento, relação, próximo passo, open source, auditável, privacidade.

- **How we handle hard things:** Name the limitation plainly. Never imply the product can resolve a relationship or replace a professional. Admit uncertainty and show the boundary between guidance and judgment.
- **Signature devices:** Open with a concrete human moment; use short paragraphs; contrast impulse vs. considered action; explain the mechanism only after the situation is clear; use sentence case; no exclamation-heavy copy; emojis rare and functional; hashtags optional and limited to 2–4 relevant terms.
- **Voice by platform:** Facebook: warm, accessible, community-first, practical. LinkedIn: slightly more structured and strategic. GitHub/TabNews: precise, transparent and technically reproducible. Communities/Reddit: helpful peer, no campaign voice or unsolicited pitch.

## Operational defaults (tool skills read these)
- **AI & synthetic media policy:** AI-assisted copy and generated visuals are allowed when they are accurate and reviewed. Do not fabricate testimonials, user stories or performance metrics. Disclose synthetic media when a realistic person, voice or event could be mistaken for real. No voice clones or avatars of real people without documented consent. Prefer diagrams, screen recordings and clearly illustrative visuals.
- **Accessibility defaults:** Alt text on every image; captions on every video; high contrast; readable type; avoid text-only meaning in color; plain language; keep important cover text inside safe margins.
- **Visual identity:** Green `#2f6f5b`, dark ink `#1c2530`, warm beige `#faf8f4`, coral accent `#c65f3f`, soft green `#e7f0ec`; see `marketing/assets/` for current Facebook assets.

## Guardrails
- **Banned words/topics:** No therapeutic, medical or guaranteed-outcome claims; no “AI replaces leaders”; no romantic framing for the product; no invented customers, metrics or endorsements.
- **Compliance / legal:** Respect privacy and consent. Never publish real conversation text or personal data without explicit permission and anonymization. Make clear that guidance is hypothetical and not professional therapy or legal advice.
- **Competitor rules:** Do not attack Claude, ChatGPT, Anthropic or other assistants. Explain compatibility and limitations accurately. Do not imply official endorsement.
- **Sensitivity:** Conflict, family, mental health, layoffs, grief and workplace power dynamics require extra care; use fictionalized examples and avoid diagnosing people.

## Show, don't tell — example posts
- **Loves:** The landing contrast between an impulsive reply and a fact-based next step — it makes the behavioral change visible without promising an outcome.
- **Loves:** “A orientação é sempre uma hipótese, nunca uma prescrição. Você decide.” — it states the core ethical boundary in plain language.
- **Loves:** “Treinamento de liderança evapora em duas semanas.” — it names the market problem directly and creates a reason for reinforcement.
- **Hates:** Generic AI launch copy such as “Revolutionize your leadership with cutting-edge AI.” — empty hype, no situation or proof.
- **Hates:** Posts that present a generated response as universally correct — they violate the product’s central belief in human judgment.
